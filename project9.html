Booking lab tests for the whole family, in one cart

A mobile booking journey that brings employer benefits, doctor prescriptions, family members, home collection and lab visits together — so members can book the right tests, at the right place, without working out who pays for what.

The product challenge

A lab order is rarely one test, for one person, at one place.

A single booking can mix an employer-sponsored annual health check, tests a doctor prescribed last week, a paid add-on and a test for a parent. Some samples are collected at home; ECG, X-ray, eye and dental checks need a lab. Each person has different benefits, and each lab can do a different set of tests.

The challenge was to design one booking journey where members always know who a test is for, where it happens, when to prepare, and exactly what they will pay — without turning the cart into a spreadsheet.

My role and scope

I led the end-to-end booking experience: the core flow, collection modes, the "book later" lab visit, one-tap repeat health checks, sponsored-check upsell for family cohorts, post-payment order details and the home-screen visibility rules that decide which card a member sees first. The work spans five flows and more than 70 screens and bottom sheets, plus a shared component and image set.

Key design decisions

Context first

A home that knows why you are here

An upcoming health check, a prescription from a recent consult, unused family benefits or a report to review — explicit visibility rules decide which card leads.

People, not SKUs

The cart is grouped by member

Each person shows sponsored and self-paid items separately, so "Free" and "₹499" never blur together.

Place is a choice

Collection mode, recommended

Home collection plus a lab visit is split automatically (68 tests at home, 4 at a lab) with no extra charge, or everything at one lab.

Decision: tell members what a lab cannot do

Labs differ in what they offer. Instead of hiding incompatible labs, the list explains each limitation ("Chest X-ray not available here"), keeps prices identical across partner labs and, when no nearby lab can do every test, guides the member through a two-lab booking with a second lab pinned and same-day slots suggested.

Principle: a constraint the member can see is a decision they can make. A constraint they discover at the lab is a failed visit.

Decision: let preparation shape the schedule

Slot selection carries the clinical context of the tests in the cart: fasting packages favour early-morning slots, the lab visit is sequenced after the fasting blood sample, quieter weekdays carry a small discount, and members can collect samples now and book the lab visit later. The confirmation turns this into a timeline with a fasting reminder for the night before.

Decision: make every rupee traceable

Payment stacks an employer benefit, an OPD wallet, a weekday coupon and self-paid items. The review screen lists both appointments, then walks from the test total to the net payable line by line, so a ₹0 order is visibly ₹0 — and members understand why.

Designing beyond the happy path

No lab nearby

Offer to request a lab or switch to home collection.

Address not serviceable

Explain before slots, not after payment.

No slots on a day

Suggest the nearest day with availability.

Partial lab

Confirm the split and pick a second lab.

Office camp

Choose a company camp and time instead of an address.

Payment failed

Keep the covered part booked and retry only the paid part.

Extending the system: repeat checks and family

One-tap annual check

When the yearly check is due, the home card pre-fills last year's package, lab and time, so a repeat booking takes a single tap — with "Change" one step away.

Sponsored checks for family

Cohort-aware flows for employee-only, employee + spouse and "Book one, gift one" reuse the same cart, slots and payment steps rather than creating parallel journeys.

High-fidelity visual design

Warm photography, one accent, no surprises

The showcase set pairs real health photography with a single blue accent on neutral surfaces. Prices always show the benefit beside the original amount, and every state carries a label — never colour alone.

Reflection

The hardest part was not any single screen; it was keeping four variables — person, test, place and payer — understandable at every step. Grouping by member, recommending a collection mode and showing each lab's limits up front turned a complex order into a sequence of small, confident choices.
